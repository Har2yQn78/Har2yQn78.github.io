Twenty-sixth.

That's the whole thing. Out of everyone on PS4 who ever finished *Assassin's Creed Valhalla* down to the last trophy, twenty-five people got there before me and nobody else did. It's almost six years old now, that number, and it has never once been useful to me. I still like it more than most things I've done on purpose.

## I didn't even like the game

Let me get the dishonest version out of the way first, the one where I loved every hour. I didn't.

Valhalla stopped feeling like Assassin's Creed to me somewhere in the first ten hours. The series I grew up on was about being a small sharp thing in a big city, a rooftop and a crowd and one person you were there for. Valhalla is a Viking RPG wearing the name. Gear tiers, power levels, a skill web the size of a wall. Not bad, just not the thing I came for.

And then it repeats. You arrive in a region, you meet the local powers, someone is scheming, you pick a side, there's a betrayal, you resolve it, you sail to the next region and do it again. The first time it lands. The fourth time you can see the shape of it from the loading screen. England is enormous and most of that enormousness is the same three hours wearing different weather.

So that's the honest starting position. I was not on a pilgrimage. I was in a game I found repetitive, and I decided to finish all of it anyway.

## The window

Here's the part that made it worth doing.

Trophy leaderboards have a thing most players never think about: they're chronological. Every platinum ever earned sits in a list ordered by when it popped, and the top of that list is decided in the first days after launch and then frozen forever. Nobody takes 26th place away from you in 2031. That slot closes when it closes.

I saw that window and I wanted to be inside it. That's the entire motivation. Not the trophy, which anybody can get eventually, and thousands have. The position. A tiny, unsanctioned, completely private race against people I'd never meet, with no prize and no announcement, that would be over in about a week whether I entered or not.

Nobody asked me to run it. That's most of why I did.

## What the race actually was

It wasn't hard. I want to be exact about that, because the flattering version of this story would make it sound like a skill check, and it wasn't one. Valhalla's platinum has almost no difficulty in it. There's no boss that walls you, no timing you have to drill, no run you have to be good enough to survive.

It's hours. That's the whole obstacle. Hours and hours of them.

Every region cleared. Every wealth marker, every mystery, every artifact, on a map that keeps unrolling past the point where you thought it ended. The settlement built up. Orlog learned properly, because you don't get to skip the dice game. The long arcs finished, including the ones I'd already mentally checked out of. And all of it under a clock, because the whole point was speed, so there was no drifting, no wandering off to look at something, no putting it down for a night because I was bored. I was bored constantly. I kept going constantly.

That's a strange way to spend a week. Racing to the end of something you're not enjoying, at maximum effort, for a rank that will appear on exactly one webpage.

## The pop

It ended the way these always end, which is anticlimactically.

The last trophy fired, and half a second later the platinum came in behind it — [*Viking Legend*](https://psnprofiles.com/trophy/11736-assassins-creed-valhalla/1-viking-legend) — and the screen did its little animation, and that was it. No fanfare. The game just kept sitting there, waiting for me to keep playing, which I never did again.

Then I went and checked the list, and the number was 26.

I didn't tell anyone. There was no one to tell, really — it isn't a thing you can explain at a table without it sounding either boastful or deranged. I just sat there with it for a minute. Some private counter I'd set for myself had come back green, and that was enough, and then I went to bed.

## So

Almost six years later the number is still there, and it'll still be there long after anyone cares what game it was attached to. I've done harder things since. I've done more important things, definitely. But there's something clean about that one that the important things don't have.

Valhalla wasn't a one-off either. I've had the account since 2016, ten years of it now, and trophy hunting is one of the very few hobbies I've kept that whole time without ever getting tired of it — somewhere around 300 platinums as I write this, all of them on [psnprofiles](https://psnprofiles.com/Har2y_Qn78) if you want to see the pile. Most of them I loved playing. This one I didn't, which is exactly why it's the one I still bring up.

I didn't like the game. I didn't get anything for it. The window was open for about a week, I climbed through it, and it shut behind me for good.

Twenty-sixth. Still mine.
